About

Physicist at the edge of absolute zero

I am pursuing an International Doctorate in Atomic and Molecular Photonics at the European Laboratory for Non-Linear Spectroscopy (LENS), University of Florence, Italy (Nov 2023 – present). My work is embedded in the COMOMET programme (Fundamental Physical Metrology with Cold Molecules) at CNR-INO, Italy.

My experimental work focuses on constructing a Magneto-Optical Trap (MOT) for CaF molecules. I have also visited the Laboratoire de Physique des Lasers, Paris and Imperial College London, working on optical pumping and precision spectroscopy of cold polar molecules.

Before my PhD, I was a Junior Research Fellow at IUCAA, Pune, synchronising optical atomic clocks using ultra-stable fibre optic channels — work resulting in a published patent (co-inventor). I am UGC NET and JRF qualified (Government of India, 2021).

Research

Pushing molecules to quantum limits

01

Buffer Gas Cooling

I develop cryogenic helium buffer gas cells to produce slow, cold molecular beams of CaF and CO at ~4 K. My work on a continuous CO molecular beam was published in Optics Express (2025), characterising beam properties via metastable CO spectroscopy.

02

Laser Cooling of Molecules

At CNR-INO Florence I am constructing a Magneto-Optical Trap for CaF — engineering highly-closed photon-cycling transitions with multi-frequency repump schemes and optical pumping to prepare molecules in magnetically trappable states.

03

Quantum Simulation

Arrays of ultracold polar molecules in optical lattices and tweezers offer a powerful platform for analog quantum simulation of strongly correlated many-body physics, topological phases, and quantum magnetism inaccessible to classical computation.

04

Quantum Computing

Polar molecules in optical tweezers are a frontier qubit platform. Their rich internal rotational structure and long-range dipolar interactions enable high-fidelity entangling gates and qudit-based quantum error correction schemes.

05

Ultracold Chemistry

At microkelvin temperatures, chemical reactions are governed purely by quantum mechanics — tunnelling, resonances, and partial-wave scattering dominate. Ultracold polar molecules allow controlled studies of reaction dynamics, stereochemistry, and quantum-state-selected collisions.

06

Physics Beyond the Standard Model

Precision spectroscopy of cold polyatomic molecules — particularly their vibrational and rotational transitions — provides exquisite sensitivity to variation of fundamental constants and new CP-violating forces, offering a tabletop window into physics beyond the Standard Model.

Science Dissemination

Public engagement

Four-Rod Linear Paul Trap Toy Model · National Science Day 2023
Designed, built, and demonstrated a four-rod linear Paul trap toy model that successfully trapped charged particles (molecular ions), showcased during the National Science Day celebration at IUCAA, Pune. The Paul trap uses oscillating electric fields applied to four symmetrically arranged rods to confine ions in free space — a foundational technology in quantum computing and mass spectrometry.
